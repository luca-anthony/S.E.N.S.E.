PROJECT TITLE: HaptiSight - Multi-Array Wearable Spatial-Depth-to-Tactile Interface for Visually Impaired Navigation

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS
Standard white canes and guide dogs leave a critical gap: they primarily identify ground-level obstacles directly in front of the user. Head-height hazards (low tree branches, signposts, open cabinet doors, suspended overhangs) remain undetectable until contact occurs. Existing electronic travel aids (ETAs) often rely on single-point ultrasonic sensors that trigger audio beeps. Audio alerts pollute critical environmental hearing cues, while single-point measurements fail to convey spatial context or object geometry.

This project develops an integrated wearable system (a chest harness or forearm sleeve) featuring a 3x3 matrix of Time-of-Flight (ToF) distance sensors paired with a corresponding 3x3 matrix of haptic vibration motors. By mapping 3D spatial depth arrays into real-time tactile intensity and frequency patterns on the skin, the device translates environmental geometry into touch perception without requiring visual sight or compromising hearing.

Hypothesis: Implementing a non-linear, edge-detecting haptic mapping algorithm will reduce obstacle navigation times and collision frequencies by at least 40% compared to traditional linear single-sensor distance-to-vibration feedback mechanisms.

2. HARDWARE AND SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE (ENGINEERING TEAM)
The engineering team handles component selection, power distribution, circuit design, signal multiplexing, and wearable ergonomics.

A. Microcontroller Unit (MCU):

* Primary MCU: ESP32-S3 or Teensy 4.1. Preferred due to high clock speeds, hardware I2C/SPI buses, low power consumption, and dual-core processing capabilities (allowing core 0 to process sensor inputs while core 1 manages haptic motor outputs).

B. Sensor Matrix (3x3 Grid):

* Sensor Type: VL53L1X Time-of-Flight (ToF) laser-ranging sensors (or Ultrasonic JSN-SR04T / MB1010 for wider cone sensing).
* Arrangement: 3 rows by 3 columns mounted on a rigid/flexible chest matrix covering three elevation zones:
* Top Row (3 sensors): Overhead and head-height field-of-view (1.5 m to 2.2 m above ground).
* Middle Row (3 sensors): Mid-torso and waist-height field-of-view (0.7 m to 1.5 m).
* Bottom Row (3 sensors): Low-level hazards, steps, and curbs (0 m to 0.7 m).


* Multiplexing: Since VL53L1X sensors share a default I2C address, use a TCA9548A 8-channel I2C multiplexer (or two multiplexers daisy-chained) to query each sensor rapidly without address conflicts.

C. Haptic Actuator Array:

* Motors: Linear Resonant Actuators (LRAs) or Eccentric Rotating Mass (ERM) coin vibration motors (e.g., 10mm x 2.0mm).
* Drivers: DRV2605L haptic motor driver chips connected via I2C, or transistor/MOSFET driver arrays (e.g., ULN2003 or custom MOSFET gates) controlled via Pulse-Width Modulation (PWM).
* Layout: Worn against a skin surface with high tactile resolution, such as the inner forearm, upper chest, or upper back.

D. Power Management & Mechanicals:

* Power: 3.7V LiPo battery (2000mAh+) feeding a step-up/step-down buck boost regulator supplying clean 3.3V and 5.0V rails.
* Chassis: Custom 3D-printed modular sensor housing featuring 15-degree angled mounts for the top and bottom sensor rows to maximize vertical field-of-view.

3. SOFTWARE AND ALGORITHMIC DESIGNS (CODING TEAM)
The coding team handles signal acquisition, noise filtering, spatial algorithms, and haptic rendering curves.

A. Data Pipeline and Filtering:

1. Matrix Polling: Continuous sequential polling of all 9 sensors via the I2C multiplexer at a target refresh rate of 30 Hz to 50 Hz.
2. Noise Suppression: Apply a moving median filter combined with a low-pass exponential filter to remove false reflections, specular noise, and outdoor sun-glare anomalies.

B. Experimental Feedback Algorithms (To Be Compared):

* Mode 1: Linear Direct Distance Mapping
* Formula: V_out = V_max * (1 - (D_measured / D_max))
* Vibration intensity scales proportionally with distance. Simple, but quickly leads to skin desensitization.


* Mode 2: Logarithmic Skin-Adapted Scaling
* Human tactile perception follows Weber-Fechner laws (non-linear sensitivity).
* Formula: V_out = V_max * (log(D_max / max(D_measured, D_min)) / log(D_max / D_min))
* Provides subtle feedback for distant objects while rapidly ramping up intensity as an obstacle enters the immediate safety zone (under 1 meter).


* Mode 3: Velocity-Sensitive Closure Rate Mode
* Calculates delta distance over time (dD/dt).
* Actuators remain silent for stationary distant objects, but activate with high intensity if the rate of closure toward an obstacle indicates imminent collision.


* Mode 4: Spatial Edge & Silhouette Detection
* The MCU calculates spatial gradients across neighboring matrix cells: G_x = |D(x+1, y) - D(x, y)| and G_y = |D(x, y+1) - D(x, y)|.
* Vibrate actuators only where a significant depth discontinuity occurs, allowing the wearer to feel the boundary, door opening, or outline of an object.



4. EXPERIMENTAL TESTING METHODOLOGY (SCIENCE FAIR DATA)
To qualify as an advanced science fair submission, quantitative performance metrics must be collected across multiple experimental trials.

A. Variables:

* Independent Variable: Haptic algorithm type (Linear, Logarithmic, Velocity-Sensitive, Edge-Detection) and sensor configuration.
* Dependent Variables:
1. Time to navigate a standardized 15-meter obstacle course (seconds).
2. Total number of physical collisions (head, torso, ground).
3. Spatial recognition accuracy (percentage of correct identification of mock obstacles: doorway, pole, low hanging branch, box on floor).


* Controlled Variables: Course layout, obstacle dimensions, walking path width, ambient lighting, and ear protection/masking noise worn by subjects to prevent reliance on acoustic echoes.

B. Testing Protocol:

1. Construct a standardized, repeatable indoor/outdoor maze containing 10 hazards (3 head-height, 4 torso-height, 3 ground-level).
2. Run trials with blindfolded test participants across each feedback mode (minimum 10 trials per mode per participant).
3. Record time, collisions, and subjective spatial mapping accuracy ratings.
4. Calculate statistical significance using ANOVA or paired T-tests to determine which algorithm offers the best balance of speed, safety, and cognitive load reduction.
5. REQUIRED COMPONENTS AND SHOPPING LIST

* Microcontroller: ESP32-S3 Dev Board or Teensy 4.1
* Distance Sensors: 9x VL53L1X Time-of-Flight sensors
* I2C Multiplexer: 2x TCA9548A modules
* Haptic Drivers: 9x DRV2605L breakout boards (or custom MOSFET driver board)
* Haptic Motors: 9x ERM coin vibration motors (10mm)
* Power: 3.7V 2500mAh LiPo battery + TP4056 charging board + 5V step-up boost converter
* Framing: Flexible chest harness/vest, 3D printing filament (PLA/TPU), jumper wires, breadboards/perfboards
